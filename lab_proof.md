## Phase 1: System brief

**What it does.** The system checks the facts about one university programme and writes a report for a Turkish student and their family. It finds the programme's official web pages, reads them, and pulls out key facts: tuition, deadlines, language and academic requirements, scholarships. Each fact is stored with the exact sentence it came from and a link. Plain computer rules (not the AI) then decide whether each fact counts as Verified, Needs Review, Inferred or Unknown. It also adds national admission and recognition rules from a small library of official documents, converts costs into Turkish lira, and compares the requirements with the student's profile.

**Inputs.**
- The programme name, university and target intake.
- Public web pages (university sites, official portals) and 15 public reference documents.
- A student profile: grades, language test scores, budget, citizenship. **In the MVP this profile is synthetic.** In production it would be a real student's data, often a minor's, which is personal data.

**Outputs.** A one-page report and a machine-readable record. It gives facts with a status and a source, a total yearly cost, a list of "met / gap / check" items (e.g. "German C1 needed, you have B1"), and next steps. It does **not** give a score, a ranking, an admission prediction or a decision.

**Who is affected.** Students and their families, who may decide whether to apply, or how to spend money and time preparing, based on the report. Indirectly: the universities described.

**Human review.** In the MVP, no one reviews the report before the student sees it. The student and family read it and decide what to do. The universities make every admission decision. The AI only reads pages; the status of each fact is decided by rules, and the report is assembled by code, not written by the AI.

**Who built it.** I built it alone. It uses third-party services: OpenAI (a general-purpose language model for reading pages), Tavily (web search), Jina (page reading), Pinecone (document library), Frankfurter (exchange rates), and n8n, Telegram and Airtable for delivery and logging.

**Who would use it in production.** Unioptt (my own platform) would offer it to students and families. Later, school counsellors and private high schools could use it to advise their students.

*(≈ 450 words)*

## Phase 2: Risk tier classification

| Question | My answer |
|---|---|
| Does this system fall under any prohibited category (Art. 5)? | **No.** No manipulation, no exploitation of vulnerabilities, no social scoring, no biometrics or emotion recognition. One watch point: users include minors (an Art. 5(1)(b) vulnerable group), so the report must not pressure or mislead them. |
| Does it operate in any of the eight Annex III areas? | **Close to one: Annex III point 3, education.** It does not determine admission (3a), evaluate learning outcomes (3b) or monitor exams (3d). The closest is **3(c), assessing the level of education a person can access**, because the "readiness & gaps" section compares a person's profile with entry requirements. |
| If Annex III: does it "significantly influence" decisions, or is it narrow/preparatory? | **Narrow and preparatory (Art. 6(3)).** It collects and checks public facts and shows where the student meets or misses published requirements. It does not decide anything; universities do. **But:** Art. 6(3) does not protect systems that perform **profiling** of natural persons. In production, comparing a real student's profile with requirements may count as profiling. ⚖️ |
| Does it interact with users or generate content requiring disclosure (Art. 50)? | **Not strictly.** It is not a chatbot, and the report is assembled by code, not generated by the AI. The general-purpose model's obligations sit with OpenAI. A voluntary AI disclosure is still recommended (see gaps). |
| First-pass risk tier | **Minimal risk, with a borderline flag toward high-risk (Annex III 3(c)) for production use with real student profiles.** |
| One-sentence justification | The agent checks public programme facts and shows rule-based gaps without deciding access, so it falls outside Annex III 3 under the preparatory-task exception of Art. 6(3); this depends on the profile comparison not counting as profiling. |

**Ambiguity and what would resolve it.** The tier depends on (1) whether the readiness comparison counts as **profiling** under GDPR Art. 4(4) and Art. 6(3) AI Act, and (2) whether, in production, it moves from "showing published requirements" to "telling a student which education they can access". If it ever produced an eligibility verdict ("you will / won't be admitted") or a score, it would likely become high-risk. A lawyer should confirm. ⚖️

## Phase 5: Gap analysis and remediation plan

**Gap 1: The "Verified" label can mislead (consumer protection, Art. 50 spirit)**
- **Current state:** "Verified" means the agent's automatic checks passed. A family will read it as "a person confirmed this". No human checks most facts; the README explains this, the report does not.
- **Required state:** information given to consumers must not be misleading (EU unfair commercial practices rules), and users should understand what an AI-assisted result means.
- **Remediation:** rename to **"Auto-verified"** in the report; add a short legend ("checked automatically against the official page; confirm before applying"); keep "Verified" only for facts a person confirmed, with a `verified_by` field.
- **Escalation:** No.

**Gap 2: No AI disclosure in the report**
- **Current state:** the report and the Telegram summary do not say that AI was used to read sources.
- **Required state:** not strictly required by Art. 50 (the text is assembled by code), but good practice and expected by families, especially for minors.
- **Remediation:** a two-line notice at the top of every report (see `transparency_notice.md`).
- **Escalation:** No.

**Gap 3: The classification depends on "profiling"**
- **Current state:** the readiness section compares a student's grades, language level and budget with requirements. With synthetic data this is harmless; with real students it may count as profiling, which removes the Art. 6(3) exception.
- **Required state:** documented assessment of why the system is not high-risk (Art. 6(4)), or the full high-risk obligations.
- **Remediation:** write the Art. 6(4) assessment; keep the readiness logic as transparent rules showing published requirements side by side, with no score, verdict or ranking; let the student choose whether to add their profile at all.
- **Escalation:** **Yes, a lawyer.** ⚖️

**Gap 4: Real student data, minors and US vendors (GDPR / KVKK)**
- **Current state:** the MVP uses a synthetic profile. Production would process minors' data, and the stack uses US services (OpenAI, Pinecone, Tavily, Airtable).
- **Required state:** legal basis and parental consent for under-18s (GDPR Art. 8, KVKK), data minimisation, processor agreements, a transfer mechanism for US vendors, and a retention policy.
- **Remediation:** keep the student profile **out of every external service**: matching runs in local Python, and only programme facts go to the LLM (to confirm in code); move run logs from Airtable to an EU-hosted store; define retention; update the privacy notice.
- **Escalation:** **Yes, a data protection officer.** ⚖️

**Gap 5: No human check before the report reaches a student**
- **Current state:** the report goes straight to the user; errors (e.g. a fee payment deadline taken for an application deadline) are caught only if a check fails.
- **Required state:** not required at minimal risk, but becomes required (Art. 14) if reclassified, and is the main protection against wrong facts.
- **Remediation:** for production, a review queue: any report with Needs Review facts, or any fact with a large change from the last run, is checked by a person before release; overrides are logged.
- **Escalation:** No.

**Gap 6: LLM paraphrase of country rules is not checked**
- **Current state:** Python checks that each quote is real, but not that the paraphrase says no more than the quote (e.g. a scholarship described as "available to non-EU students" when the source only names it).
- **Required state:** accurate information to consumers.
- **Remediation:** show the quote instead of the paraphrase for country rules, or add a check that key terms in the paraphrase appear in the quote; keep "check eligibility for Turkish citizens".
- **Escalation:** No.

**Also applies:** AI literacy (Art. 4) for anyone at Unioptt, schools or counsellors using the system, in force since February 2025.

## Reinforce

**A component I glossed over in Project 3:** the readiness & gaps section. I treated it as simple arithmetic ("C1 needed, B1 given"). Through a regulatory lens it is the one part that evaluates a person, and with real students it is what could push the system toward Annex III education. The compliance picture changes from "minimal" to "borderline" once I include it honestly.

**A design decision that creates a compliance burden:** calling automatic checks "Verified". It was the natural word, because Unioptt already used it, but in Unioptt's backend it means a human confirmed the data. Reusing it for an automatic result blurs exactly the line the EU AI Act and consumer law care about. In hindsight I would have used "Auto-verified" from the start.

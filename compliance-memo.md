# Compliance memo: AI Entrepreneur Coach

**To:** Head of Product
**From:** Zahra Moghaddasi
**Subject:** EU AI Act, first-pass compliance review

## System classification

AI Entrepreneur Coach falls under the limited risk tier of the EU AI Act, because it talks to users directly and writes AI generated text, which triggers the transparency duties in Article 50. It does not fall under any banned use in Article 5, and it does not fall under any high-risk area in Annex III, since no employer, school, or other institution uses it to decide anything about another person. Users are only ever deciding for themselves.

## Role map

I am the provider, since I built the system and made the code public for others to use. There is no deployer standing between me and most users today, since people running their own copy for their own decision counts as personal use under the Act, not deployment. OpenAI and Cohere, the AI services the system relies on, carry their own separate compliance duties as model providers. Pinecone, the storage layer, sits outside the AI Act and is relevant mainly for data handling under GDPR.

## Key findings

1. The app does not currently tell users clearly that they are talking to an AI system before they submit their CV and personality answers. The product name hints at it, but the Act asks for a clear, in app statement.
2. The exported reports, including the fit explanations and the 90 day roadmap, do not say anywhere that the text was written by AI.
3. The app collects real personal data (a CV and personality answers), sends it to OpenAI, Cohere, and Pinecone, and gives no privacy notice, no retention rule, and no way to request deletion. This is a GDPR gap, not an AI Act gap, but it needs to be fixed alongside the AI Act work.

## Recommended next steps

1. Add a short, plain notice on the app's first screen stating that AI is used to read the profile and generate suggestions. Low effort, do this first.
2. Add a line to every exported report stating the text was generated by AI and should be reviewed by the user before acting on it. Also low effort.
3. Write a short privacy notice covering what data is collected, which third parties process it, and how long it is kept. This step needs input from someone with GDPR knowledge, since the data is sent to processors outside the EU.
4. Before this system is ever offered to an organisation to use on behalf of other people (rather than individuals using it on themselves), redo this classification. That kind of use could move the system into a high-risk Annex III category and would need a fresh legal review.

## Caveats

This memo is a first-pass, non-legal review. It is not a legal opinion, not a formal conformity assessment, and not a certification of compliance. The classification and gaps above should be confirmed by a qualified lawyer or data protection specialist before the system is used at any real scale, especially the international data transfer question raised in the GDPR gap.

# Stretch

## Note on Approach

The lab's Stretch instructions ask for an artifact closing "the single most significant gap identified." This audit identified zero formal gaps — the system is minimal risk and the one borderline question (Article 50 transparency) resolved to a confirmed non-issue. Rather than skip Stretch or manufacture an artificial gap, the artifact below strengthens the one area that was genuinely closest to a judgment call: making the report's existing self-disclosure more explicit and complete, so the "non-issue" conclusion stays robustly true even under a stricter future reading of Article 50, rather than resting on the current footer alone.

## Artifact: Transparency Notice for End Users

*Proposed addition to the top of every generated report, above the Executive Summary — not a compliance requirement given the minimal-risk classification, but a low-cost strengthening of the system's existing self-disclosure practice.*

---

> **About this report**
>
> This should-cost analysis was generated by an AI-assisted tool. Here's what that means in practice:
>
> - **All dollar figures, weights, and distances in this report are calculated by deterministic software** — fixed formulas and verified data sources — not estimated or written by AI. These numbers do not vary between runs of the same inputs.
> - **The narrative sections** (Executive Summary, Design & Cost Advisory Notes, Confidence & Assumptions) **are written by an AI language model**, grounded in a curated reference library of manufacturing knowledge and the specific part details provided. These sections explain and contextualize the numbers above — they do not introduce new figures.
> - **This report is a should-cost estimate for negotiation support**, not a certified quote, an engineering specification, or a guarantee of actual production cost. It should be reviewed by a qualified buyer or engineer before being used in a supplier negotiation.
> - Questions about how a specific figure was calculated, or which assumptions were used, are available on request from [Jay Ramamoorthi / AI Consulting Engagement].

---

This is intentionally short — a few sentences, not a legal disclaimer wall — since the goal is genuine clarity for the reader, not defensive over-hedging. It makes explicit three things the current footer only implies: which parts of the report are deterministic versus AI-written, that the report is advisory rather than authoritative, and who to ask for more detail. Adding this costs one paragraph of report-generation logic and closes the gap between "the report technically discloses its automated origin" and "an ordinary reader would clearly understand what that means for how much to trust each section."

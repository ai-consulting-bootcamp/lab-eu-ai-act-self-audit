# MIRA role map

## Role mapping under the AI Act

The local MIRA source shows a private, authenticated creative tool combining model-generated outputs and user-directed brand-world generation. In a first-pass assessment, the roles should be mapped as follows.

| Role | Entity | Key AI Act obligations |
|---|---|---|
| Provider | The MIRA product team / system builder that developed and configured the creative AI workflow | Ensure the system is appropriately documented, provide information to users, and manage relevant model-provider and tool integration responsibilities if the system is deployed externally |
| Deployer | The organisation or owner using MIRA in a professional or client-facing context | Apply user-facing transparency, human oversight, lawful use of any personal data, and operational controls in the actual deployment |
| Vendor / model provider | External model or image-generation services used to generate the moodboard and visual directions | Their own provider obligations, where relevant, depend on how they are integrated and whether they are a separate provider for the AI component |

## Practical analysis for MIRA

The system is best viewed as a provider-side built system with a deployer environment that is private and authentication-gated. This means the product team has a provider-like role in the exercise, especially if they configure the AI workflow and build the end-user journey. The deployer role becomes important if the system is used by a client or inside a business context beyond the private internal staging environment.

## Why this matters

The local source shows a clear human-review point and owner-scoped flow, which reduces risk compared with fully automated decisions. However, provider/deployer boundaries still matter because the system directly generates content, depends on external model providers, and would need a clear operational description if it were placed in a real production environment.

## Important caveat

This is a first-pass role mapping, not a legal determination. Legal review would be needed to confirm precise allocation of provider vs deployer duties when the system is used beyond private staging or when external vendors are involved in a commercial deployment.

# CompanyScope classification, roles and remediation

**Assessment date:** 16 November 2026
**Scope:** Intended EU professional use of the published Project 3 build. No production access, vendor contract review or legal sign-off.
**Evidence baseline:** [CompanyScope commit 542665b](https://github.com/sean173-lang/CompanyScope/tree/542665b0a4c6c5c18a8ac59896b87d5bd0eb39f6), the bundled workflow, saved report and offline replay.

## 1. Classification

| Question | First-pass answer |
| --- | --- |
| Prohibited practice under Article 5? | None identified. The workflow collects company evidence and generates draft text. It does not infer emotions, perform biometric categorization or score people socially. |
| Annex III or Article 6(1) high-risk? | No identified Annex III intended use and no identified Annex I regulated-product safety component. Private company research by legal teams is not Annex III 8(a), which concerns specified uses by or on behalf of judicial authorities and similar alternative dispute resolution uses. There is no intended creditworthiness scoring of natural persons or applicant evaluation. |
| Significant influence or narrow/preparatory task? | Not applicable. The conclusion rests on intended purpose outside Annex III, not an Article 6(3) exception. A new use such as ranking job applicants or assessing a natural person's creditworthiness must be reassessed on its own facts. |
| Article 50(2) synthetic outputs? | Yes, first-pass. It generates substantive analysis and follow-up suggestions, not just formatting or standard editing. Internal use by a professional client does not itself remove this provider duty. The fixed report headings and source-derived register fields do not change the character of the generated passages. |
| Article 50(1) direct interaction and (5) timing? | Probably not for the current one-shot lookup form, with no genuine conversational exchange, applying the Commission guidance. Confirm with counsel because the user sends a request and receives a personalized report. The existing form mentions AI-assisted analysis before submission. Keep and strengthen that notice regardless. Reassess if interactive questions or follow-ups are added. Where applicable, notice must be accessible and clear no later than first interaction or exposure. |
| Article 50(4) publication? | Not triggered by the intended internal use. Reassess if a deployer publishes generated text to inform the public on matters of public interest. The exception requires human review or editorial control and an identified person or entity holding editorial responsibility. The public evidence sample is a product demo, not permission to publish future client reports. |
| First-pass tier | **Limited risk / transparency** in the lab taxonomy. Article 50(2) is the clearest obligation. No high-risk checklist applies to this use. |

Article 50 generally applies from 2 August 2026. The 2 December 2026 Article 50(2) transition applies to eligible systems already placed on the market before 2 August 2026. No evidence supports that exception for this build. Confirm whether a planned delivery constitutes placing on the market or putting into service. Do not infer that the bootcamp demo alone establishes either. This assessment was formed against the law first and cross-checked against the Commission's Article 50 guidance. The interactive checker was not used to produce a separate result. [1–4]

## 2. Role map

| Role | Entity for proposed rollout | Key responsibilities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finished-system provider | Sean or the entity putting CompanyScope into service or marketing it under its own name. If the client commissions and puts it into service under its own name, revisit the identity rather than assuming the developer is always the provider. | Article 50(2) marking and detectability. Article 50(1)/(5) if the interaction meets the test. Article 4 AI literacy for relevant staff and operators. Document purpose, scope and supported delivery formats. |
| Deployer | The law firm or other professional client controlling use. Its trained staff operate it. | Article 4 AI literacy. Article 50(4) if applicable after a publication decision. Set staff review and sharing rules, and determine separate GDPR responsibilities. |
| Upstream model vendor | OpenAI supplies the hosted model/API, not the completed CompanyScope system. | Confirm responsible model-provider entity and applicable GPAI obligations under Chapter V, including Article 53 where applicable. Ask what provenance or detection capability the API supplies. Downstream Article 50 duties cannot simply be assigned away. |
| Orchestration, search and data vendors | n8n orchestrates. OpenRegister supplies registry data. Tavily supplies search leads. | Vendor-specific duties depend on each product and role. No basis here to treat these components as high-risk AI or their vendors as the finished-system provider. Verify contracts, usage conditions and data handling separately. |
| Importer | None identified for the finished system in the proposed EU provider/client setup. | Reassess if an EU entity places a non-EU-branded AI system on the EU market. Using a foreign model API is not, by itself, evidence of that importer role. |

## 3. Gaps and remediation

| ID / status | Obligation or control | Current state | Required or target state | Action, owner and escalation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 · Gap | Article 50(2) marking and detectability | The form describes AI-assisted analysis, but the export and saved example show no verified output-level machine-readable marking and detection mechanism across Markdown, browser and webhook. | Mark the generated output in machine-readable form and enable detection. The solution must meet the statutory effectiveness, interoperability, robustness and reliability criteria as far as technically feasible. | Product engineering prototypes structured provenance in each supported format and an independent detector or verification method. Coordinate with the model vendor and test download, conversion, copy and partial extraction. Do not count a label or container field alone as proof. **Escalate:** AI counsel and provenance specialist before release. |
| G2 · Partial / scope to verify | Article 50(1), (5) if applicable | First screen already mentions AI assistance. No accessibility verification or recorded decision on one-shot interaction scope. | If applicable, clearly tell users at or before first interaction. Even if not, use a consistent draft label at input and output to prevent mistaken reliance. | UX owner adds the proposed notice and tests keyboard/screen-reader presentation. Verify notice on form and operator/API guidance. **Escalate:** AI counsel if interaction expands. |
| G3 · Gap for production readiness | GDPR Arts. 5, 6, 14, 28, 32 and Ch. V where applicable, plus client confidentiality | Narrow corporate form and selected model evidence help. However, the export saves all successful and failed n8n execution data and writes a report to disk. OpenAI `store:false` concerns that request option, not all vendor logging or retention. No production lifecycle or vendor terms verified. | Lawful, minimized, secure processing with defined retention, permissions, notices and transfer safeguards as applicable. | Deployment owner inventories each field and recipient, strips unnecessary personal data before APIs, replaces full payload logs with minimal events, and verifies expiry/deletion for drafts and execution records. DPO/counsel verifies controller and processor roles, lawful basis, indirect collection duties, transfer mechanism and Art. 35 DPIA need. Keep the prototype off confidential matters until approved. |
| G4 · Partial | Article 4 literacy and review before reliance, a product control rather than an Article 14 obligation here | Exact matching, source checks and warnings are implemented. Offline replay shows an unrelated claim with a valid citation still passes. No documented training or enforced release gate. | Staff understand the limits and verify material claims before reliance or sharing. | Research lead implements the accompanying review procedure and requires completion before export for external use. Hold questionable claims and record unresolved limitations. **Escalate:** counsel for adverse person-level findings, publication or changed intended purpose. |
| G5 · Release check | Publication and intended-purpose change | Internal research is the baseline. Downloads could be reused for public commentary, person scoring or consequential screening. | Keep professional research within scope. Reclassify any proposed publication or high-stakes repurposing before launch. | Product owner adds use restrictions to onboarding, keeps publishing manual and routes exceptions to legal. **Escalate:** counsel for Art. 50(4), Annex III or professional confidentiality implications. |

**Design lesson.** The initial design optimized for frictionless report delivery. Saving full executions helped debug the prototype, but creates a privacy burden when the same workflow handles real matters. A production design should use a minimized audit trail and mark reports as drafts at creation, not rely on a footer that can disappear when the report is copied.
